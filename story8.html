📖 Reading — The Stranded Whale

Snug in our new Rock House, we were not as bored as we had been the previous winter. We had many projects to keep us busy and plenty of room to work. We hung up a ship's lantern to give us light.

After Ernest and Franz put up shelves for our library, we unpacked the books we had taken from the wreck. There were books of voyages, travels and natural history. And there were history and science books, as well as fiction in several languages. There were also maps, charts and a pair of globes.

Fritz and I set up a fine workshop. The carpenter's bench was put into place. All of our tools and instruments were neatly hung on the walls.

We still needed so many things! We built shelves, tables, benches and movable steps. We also made cupboards, pegs, doorhandles and bolts. At home in Switzerland, we had taken all these things for granted! Now we had to use imagination and hard work to get the same comforts.

Working towards a set of goals gave the family purpose and helped them stay happy during the winter.

In reality, however, the more there was to do, the better it was for us. Our adventures on the island had taught me that working towards a set of goals is the main element of happiness!

We made a wide porch along the front of the cave and sheltered it with bamboo.

Sometimes we would amuse ourselves by opening chests and packages that had not yet been touched. Many treasures were brought to light. We found mirrors, wardrobes and tables with marble tops. We also found some writing tables and good chairs, several clocks and a music box. Soon our winter home was fitted up like a palace!

It seemed that the rainy season went by quickly. Time didn't hang heavy on anyone's hands. Soon it was spring again.

We were glad to be able to leave the cave. Once again we were free to roam in the open air. We crossed Jackal River one day and were walking along the coast. Then Fritz saw something on the small island near Flamingo Marsh. From our point of view, it looked like an overturned boat!

We decided to walk closer to see what we could find. It was not a boat! The enormous object turned out to be a huge whale that had become stranded and then died.

"What a huge brute! He's ever so much larger than he seemed from a distance!"

Fritz cried out in surprise. The whale was enormous, and the family immediately began thinking about how they could make use of it.

"You'd be surprised at the many ways an animal such as this can be useful," I said. "For one thing, it can supply us with oil to use in our lantern."

We immediately returned to Rock House to get the tools we would need. The next day we went back to the island near Flamingo Marsh. The whole family came along, as we had much work to do.

We had so many things to carry! We brought all the barrels and casks that we could squeeze into the boat. We also took knives and hatchets. Since the wind was with us, we soon landed close to the whale.

Its enormous size astonished my wife and our youngest boy. The creature was almost 20 metres long and about 10 metres round. Its weight could not have been less than 23,000 kilograms!

The whale was black in colour. Its huge head was about one-third the length of the entire body. The eyes were quite small — not much larger than those of an ox. Its ears were almost impossible to see.

The animal's huge jaw was nearly five metres long. The most interesting part of the great beast was the substance known as whalebone. This whalebone appeared all along both jaws. It was solid at the base, but toward the top it was split into a kind of fringe.

The tongue was quite large, soft and full of oil. Strangely, the opening of the throat was very small — only about five centimetres wide!

"Look at that narrow throat! Whatever can this great creature eat?"

Fritz was amazed by the contrast between the whale's enormous body and its tiny throat.

"Let me explain it to you," I said. "This huge animal eats just the smallest of sea creatures. Only prawns, tiny fish, lobsters and other small creatures form its diet."

The whale swims with open mouth through areas where these creatures live. It takes them into its mouth by the millions! When the mouth is full of prey, it closes its jaws. Next, it forces the water out through the whalebone — but the captured animals cannot get through the fringe. Then it can swallow the food at its leisure.

The family learned how the whale uses its whalebone to filter tiny sea creatures from the water.

"And now, boys, let me see you climb this slippery mountain of flesh. It's time to cut it up."

Fritz and Jack got started right away. First, they helped me cut away the lips, so we could get to the whalebone.

Then Ernest worked at the creature's side, cutting out big slabs of blubber. My wife and Franz helped as well as they could to put everything in casks.

It took us several days of hard work to get everything useful from the whale. We even took some of the skin to turn into leather for harnesses and such.

When we had taken all we could, we left the whale's remains to the birds of prey. With a full cargo, we set sail for land.

We were glad to get home. We needed to rest, because we had a lot of work to do the next day. A refreshing bath, clean clothes and a good supper cheered us all. That night we slept in peace.

The next morning, we began to boil the blubber in big tubs. Then we skimmed and strained the water through a coarse cloth. In this manner, we obtained an ample supply of excellent oil.

After pouring it into bottles, we stored the oil in our back storeroom. Now we had enough oil to keep our lanterns burning for many years.

📝 Summary

1. Life in Rock House

The family settles comfortably in Rock House during the rainy season. They keep busy by building furniture, shelves, cupboards and other useful objects. They also unpack books, maps and globes from the wreck and organise a workshop. Their productive routine prevents boredom.

2. Discovering the Whale

When spring arrives, the family explores the coast near Flamingo Marsh. Fritz discovers what appears to be an overturned boat, but they soon realise that it is a huge stranded whale. The father explains the whale's body, diet and special whalebone.

3. Making Use of the Whale

The whole family works together to collect useful materials from the whale. They take blubber, whalebone, skin and other materials. Back at Rock House, they boil and strain the blubber to produce oil. The oil is stored in bottles and will keep their lanterns burning for many years.

👨‍👩‍👦 Characters

Father / Narrator

He leads the whale expedition, explains the whale's biology and organises the work of collecting its useful resources.

Fritz

He discovers the stranded whale and helps cut it up. He is curious about its unusual features.

Jack

He helps Fritz and Father cut the whale and collect useful materials.

Ernest

He helps organise the library and later cuts large slabs of blubber from the whale.

Franz

He helps put up the library shelves and later assists with collecting and storing materials from the whale.

Mother

She helps the family during the whale expedition and assists with collecting and storing the useful materials.

💡 Main Theme

Resourcefulness and Teamwork in the Face of Nature's Gifts

The chapter shows how the family makes the most of what nature provides. Instead of wasting the stranded whale, they use knowledge, hard work and teamwork to turn it into useful resources.

  • Resourcefulness: The family finds many uses for the whale.
  • Teamwork: Every family member contributes to the work.
  • Knowledge: Father explains how the whale feeds.
  • Industry: The family works continuously towards useful goals.
  • Gratitude: They appreciate the resources available to them.
  • Survival: The whale provides materials that improve their life.

📚 Vocabulary

Snug

Comfortable and warm in a small, safe space.

Charts

Maps used for navigation, especially at sea.

Globes

Spherical models of Earth.

Carpenter's Bench

A sturdy worktable used for woodworking.

Bolts

Metal fasteners used to lock or secure doors.

Fringe

Threads or strips hanging loosely.

Prawns

Small sea creatures similar to shrimp.

Blubber

The thick fat layer of a whale.

Casks

Large wooden barrels used for storing liquids or solids.

Harnesses

Straps or fittings used for animals or equipment.

Lanterns

Portable lights that use oil, candles or batteries.

Coarse Cloth

Rough fabric used for filtering or straining.

Strained

Filtered or purified by removing unwanted parts.
