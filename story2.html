📖 Chapter 2: Returning to the Wreck

My wife and I woke up early. We started to talk about the business of the day.

"I think Fritz and I should return to the wreck while the sea is still calm," I said. "We must save the poor animals that are still there. We also need to get more supplies from the ship. If we don't get to them now, we may lose them entirely."

"Yes, by all means," my wife agreed. "While you're gone, the other boys and I will look for a better place to make our home. Come, let us wake the boys and get to work."

Fritz was the first to get up. He ran outside to get his dead jackal. It was cold and stiff from the night air. He propped it on its legs by the tent. The dogs growled at it, but Fritz called them off.

"It's a yellow dog!" Franz cried.

"A wolf!" Jack exclaimed.

"No, it's a striped fox," Ernest said.

"You're all partly correct," I said. "This is a jackal. It's from the same family as the dog, the wolf and the fox."

When the baby monkey saw the jackal, it hid itself in a heap of moss inside the tent. Jack soothed and comforted the little animal.

Then we sat down to a good breakfast of biscuits, butter and cheese. As we ate, I noticed that the dogs seemed very quiet. I soon discovered the cause. The night before, they'd been hurt in the fight with the jackals. They had several deep and painful wounds, especially on their necks. The dogs began to lick each other on the places they couldn't reach with their own tongues.

Suddenly Ernest had an idea. He said the dogs needed spiked collars and that he could make them. With spiked collars, he explained, they could avoid getting the same kinds of wounds again.

I told Ernest this sounded like a good idea and that he should try it. I suggested that he could make the collars from leather and use nails for the spikes. Maybe we could find supplies on the wreck.

I told Fritz that he and I would be visiting the wreck today. Next, I worked out a set of signals with my wife so we could communicate while Fritz and I were gone.

I tied a wide strip of cloth on a pole and raised it high in the air. As long as all was well, I explained to my wife, the flag should stay up. If she needed us to return, she would lower the flag and fire three shots.

Fritz and I soon left in our barrel boat, taking the monkey with us. Fritz wanted to get milk for it as soon as possible. The current quickly carried us toward the wreck.

By the time we were on board, we could hear the animals greeting us. They weren't hungry, for they still had plenty of food. They were just happy to see us. When Fritz put the monkey by one of the goats, the little animal immediately helped himself to some goat milk.

Fritz and I then put a mast and a sail on our barrel boat.

"The current that brought us here will not take us back. But that fresh breeze will!" I explained.

By the time we got the sail ready, it was getting late. We signalled our plan to stay on board for the night.

The rest of the day was spent stowing supplies on the barrel boat. We took gunpowder, guns and a whole armful of swords, daggers and knives. We also took kitchen supplies, including more pots, knives and forks.

We packed canned meats, canned soups, hams, sausages, wheat and all the other seeds and vegetables we could find.

We found some rope, nails, tools and farming supplies. Finally, nothing else could be squeezed into our little boat.

Soon it was time to sleep. Fritz fell asleep easily, but I had trouble. For hours I tossed and turned. I was worried about my wife and children, alone and unprotected, except by the dogs.

At last morning came. I looked toward shore and was happy to see my little flag still waving.

To get the animals to shore, we had to figure out a way to keep them afloat. So we made life jackets for them. Each life jacket was made of two empty barrels, one on either side of the animal. These were tied together by leather thongs. We tied a large cork underneath for buoyancy.

One by one, the animals were fitted into their jackets and pushed into the sea. As each animal bobbed back to the surface, it slowly began swimming toward the shore.

On the way, we saw a shark trying to attack one of the sheep! Fritz quickly stopped the attack by firing two shots to the beast's head.

Leaving a bloody trail, the dying animal slowly turned in the water and swam away.

At last we all reached land safely. My wife and the boys came running out to greet us.

That night we had a truly wonderful feast. This time my wife even used a tablecloth and silverware!

During dinner, she told me about her day. She and the boys had done some exploring, as they searched for a safer place.

She wanted to build a tree house among a grove of trees. High up in the air, we'd be safe from jackals and other dangerous animals. And there we'd also have shade, which would keep us cooler in the daytime.

I agreed that this was a very good idea. Our next problem was moving. How would we transport all our supplies and animals to the new location?

We'd have to cross a river to get to the grove of trees. The solution was simple-but it wouldn't be easy.

First, we'd need to build a bridge!

📝 Chapter 2 Summary

In this chapter, the father and Fritz decide to return to the wrecked ship to save the remaining animals and collect as many supplies as they can before the wreck becomes unreachable.

Before leaving, they have breakfast and notice their dogs are injured from the previous night's attack. Ernest suggests making spiked collars to protect them in future. The father also sets up a signaling system with his wife to communicate during his absence.

Onboard the ship, Fritz and his father are warmly greeted by the animals. Fritz feeds the monkey goat milk, and they gather valuable supplies, including tools, food, weapons, and farming equipment.

They sleep on the ship and prepare to return the next day. To transport the animals back to shore, they invent floating life jackets using barrels and corks. During the return, Fritz saves a sheep from a shark by shooting at it.

Upon safely reaching the shore, the family is joyfully reunited. The mother and boys had spent the day exploring and found a grove of trees suitable for building a new home in the form of a tree house.

The father agrees that this idea would keep them safe and cool. However, the challenge remains: they must now build a bridge to cross the river and move everything to their new home.

💡 Chapter 2 Theme

Survival through Ingenuity and Teamwork

The chapter highlights the family's resourcefulness, cooperation, and problem-solving in the face of survival challenges.

It also emphasizes emotional connections with animals and the importance of planning and working together.

They licked each other in places they couldn't reach themselves.

To get milk for it from the goats on the ship.
25. Why wouldn't the current help them return from the wreck?
Because the current only carried them one way, so they needed wind and a sail to return.
26. If you were building a spiked collar, what other materials could be used besides leather and nails?
Belts, rope, wire, metal scraps, or cloth strips tied tightly.
27. What tools from the wreck could help the family build a tree house?
Hammers, nails, ropes, saws, axes, and strong canvas or wood.
28. How might you improve the animal flotation devices?
Use stronger ropes, add more corks or barrels, and attach guiding lines.
29. Compare the dangers of staying on the wreck versus staying on land.
On the wreck they were safer from wild animals but exposed to the sea. On land they were more comfortable but vulnerable to attacks.
30. What does the father's sleeplessness tell us about his leadership?
He is responsible, selfless, and deeply concerned for his family's well-being.
31. Why was making the flag signal important?
It ensured communication and safety between separated family members.
32. What does the family's organization and planning suggest about their chances of survival?
It suggests strong teamwork, intelligence, and adaptability.
33. Do you think it was a good idea to take the monkey on the boat? Why or why not?
Yes, because it was dependent on them. Alternatively, no, because it was an added responsibility on a dangerous trip.
34. Should they have stayed overnight on the wreck? Defend your answer.
Yes, to gather all supplies safely. Alternatively, no, because it risked their safety and separated the family.
35. Do you agree with the mother's idea to build a tree house?
Yes, because it protects them from animals and gives them shade.
36. Invent your own method for bringing animals ashore without barrels.
Possible answers include rope rafts, inflatable sacks, floating cages, or large crates tied with vines.
37. Design a better signal system than the flag and gunshots.
Possible answers include drum codes, coloured smoke signals, mirror flashes, or torch signals at night.
38. Predict what might happen if they didn't collect the ship supplies quickly.
The wreck could sink, storms could destroy it, animals might die, or they could run out of essential tools and food.
