📖 Chapter 2: Returning to the Wreck

My wife and I woke up early. We started to talk about the business of the day.

"I think Fritz and I should return to the wreck while the sea is still calm," I said. "We must save the poor animals that are still there. We also need to get more supplies from the ship. If we don't get to them now, we may lose them entirely."

"Yes, by all means," my wife agreed. "While you're gone, the other boys and I will look for a better place to make our home. Come, let us wake the boys and get to work."

Fritz was the first to get up. He ran outside to get his dead jackal. It was cold and stiff from the night air. He propped it on its legs by the tent. The dogs growled at it, but Fritz called them off.

"It's a yellow dog!" Franz cried.

"A wolf!" Jack exclaimed.

"No, it's a striped fox," Ernest said.

"You're all partly correct," I said. "This is a jackal. It's from the same family as the dog, the wolf and the fox."

When the baby monkey saw the jackal, it hid itself in a heap of moss inside the tent. Jack soothed and comforted the little animal.

Then we sat down to a good breakfast of biscuits, butter and cheese. As we ate, I noticed that the dogs seemed very quiet. I soon discovered the cause. The night before, they'd been hurt in the fight with the jackals. They had several deep and painful wounds, especially on their necks. The dogs began to lick each other on the places they couldn't reach with their own tongues.

Suddenly Ernest had an idea. He said the dogs needed spiked collars and that he could make them. With spiked collars, he explained, they could avoid getting the same kinds of wounds again. I told Ernest this sounded like a good idea and that he should try it. I suggested that he could make the collars from leather and use nails for the spikes. Maybe we could find supplies on the wreck.

I told Fritz that he and I would be visiting the wreck today. Next, I worked out a set of signals with my wife so we could communicate while Fritz and I were gone. I tied a wide strip of cloth on a pole and raised it high in the air.

"As long as all was well, I explained to my wife, the flag should stay up. If she needed us to return, she would lower the flag and fire three shots."

Fritz and I soon left in our barrel boat, taking the monkey with us. Fritz wanted to get milk for it as soon as possible. The current quickly carried us toward the wreck.

By the time we were on board, we could hear the animals greeting us. They weren't hungry, for they still had plenty of food. They were just happy to see us. When Fritz put the monkey by one of the goats, the little animal immediately helped himself to some goat milk.

Fritz and I then put a mast and a sail on our barrel boat.

"The current that brought us here will not take us back. But that fresh breeze will!" I explained.

By the time we got the sail ready, it was getting late. We signalled our plan to stay on board for the night.

The rest of the day was spent stowing supplies on the barrel boat. We took gunpowder, guns and a whole armful of swords, daggers and knives. We also took kitchen supplies, including more pots, knives and forks.

We packed canned meats, canned soups, hams, sausages, wheat and all the other seeds and vegetables we could find.

We found some rope, nails, tools and farming supplies. Finally, nothing else could be squeezed into our little boat.

Soon it was time to sleep. Fritz fell asleep easily, but I had trouble. For hours I tossed and turned. I was worried about my wife and children, alone and unprotected, except by the dogs.

At last morning came. I looked toward shore and was happy to see my little flag still waving.

To get the animals to shore, we had to figure out a way to keep them afloat. So we made life jackets for them. Each life jacket was made of two empty barrels, one on either side of the animal. These were tied together by leather thongs. We tied a large cork underneath for buoyancy.

One by one, the animals were fitted into their jackets and pushed into the sea. As each animal bobbed back to the surface, it slowly began swimming toward the shore.

On the way, we saw a shark trying to attack one of the sheep! Fritz quickly stopped the attack by firing two shots to the beast's head. Leaving a bloody trail, the dying animal slowly turned in the water and swam away.

At last we all reached land safely. My wife and the boys came running out to greet us.

That night we had a truly wonderful feast. This time my wife even used a tablecloth and silverware!

During dinner, she told me about her day. She and the boys had done some exploring, as they searched for a safer place.

She wanted to build a tree house among a grove of trees. High up in the air, we'd be safe from jackals and other dangerous animals. And there we'd also have shade, which would keep us cooler in the daytime.

I agreed that this was a very good idea.

Our next problem was moving. How would we transport all our supplies and animals to the new location?

We'd have to cross a river to get to the grove of trees. The solution was simple — but it wouldn't be easy.

📘 Chapter 2 Summary

In this chapter, the father and Fritz decide to return to the wrecked ship to save the remaining animals and collect as many supplies as possible before the wreck becomes unreachable.

Before leaving, the family has breakfast and discovers that Turk and Juno are badly injured from the previous night's fight with the jackals. Ernest suggests making spiked collars to protect the dogs from future attacks.

The father also creates a signaling system with his wife. A raised flag means that everything is safe, while a lowered flag followed by three gunshots means that Fritz and his father must return quickly.

On the ship, Fritz and his father collect valuable supplies, including weapons, food, tools, seeds, rope, nails and farming equipment. Fritz also gives the baby monkey goat milk.

Because it becomes too late to return safely, they spend the night on the wreck. The next morning they invent floating life jackets for the animals using barrels, leather thongs and corks.

During the journey to shore, Fritz saves a sheep from a shark by shooting it. When everyone reaches land safely, the family is reunited.

The mother explains that she and the boys have found a suitable grove of trees for a new home. She suggests building a tree house high above the ground to protect the family from wild animals and provide shade.

The father agrees, but there is one major problem: the family must cross a river to reach the new home. Their next task will be to build a bridge.

🎯 Theme

Survival through Ingenuity and Teamwork

The chapter highlights the family's resourcefulness, cooperation and problem-solving in the face of survival challenges.

It also emphasizes emotional connections with animals and the importance of planning and working together.

The eldest son. Brave, responsible and helpful during the expedition to the ship.

Practical and calm. She proposes the idea of a tree house and explores a new living space.

🧒 Ernest

Creative and observant. He suggests making spiked collars for the dogs.

👦 Jack

Energetic and enthusiastic. He comforts the baby monkey.

👦 Franz

The youngest boy. Curious and expressive.

🐕 Turk and Juno

Loyal family dogs. They are injured but remain brave and protective.

The baby monkey becomes part of the family and drinks milk from the goat.

🐐 Animals

The goat and other animals are rescued from the wreck and transported safely to shore.

📚 Vocabulary

Wreck
The remains of a damaged or destroyed ship.
Supplies
Useful materials, food or equipment needed for a particular purpose.
Jackal
A wild animal related to dogs and wolves.
Spiked
Having sharp points or spikes.
Collar
A band placed around an animal's neck.
Signal
A sign or message used to communicate information.
Mast
A tall upright pole that supports a ship's sail.
Sail
A piece of material used to catch the wind and move a boat.
Gunpowder
An explosive powder used in firearms.
Buoyancy
The ability of something to float in water.
Thongs
Narrow strips of leather or another strong material.
Grove
A small group of trees growing together.
Transport
To carry people, animals or things from one place to another.
Unprotected
Not protected from danger or harm.
Resourceful
Good at finding clever ways to solve problems.
