📖 Reading — The Death of a Monster

I was seated with Fritz and my wife on the porch in front of Rock House. We were chatting pleasantly when Fritz suddenly got up. He looked off in the distance and exclaimed, “What’s that over there, Father? First it seems to coil on the ground like a cable. Then it rises up as if it were a little mast. Then it sinks and the coils move along again. It’s coming this way!”

I took my spyglass and looked through it. Just as I feared, it was an enormous snake! “It’s coming this way,” I cried. “Thank God, we’re here at Rock House. Get everyone inside, Fritz, including the animals. Then we’ll look for a chance to kill this enemy.”

I watched as the snake came closer. From time to time, it raised its head some three metres in the air and looked about. It must have been searching for prey. As the snake crossed Family Bridge, I got inside the cave with my family. We closed the door and all the windows. Our hearts beat fast as we waited.

Soon the snake was in front of the cave. We opened one window and began shooting, but all of our shots missed. Startled by the noise, the monster slipped into the reedy marsh and disappeared.

I knew then that the snake must be a boa constrictor. It was huge — about nine metres in length! I was horrified that this monster was so close to us! I told my family that we would all have to stay inside the cave. It was not safe to go outside.

For three days, we were kept in suspense and fear. I started to wonder if the boa had left the area. But the strange way our geese and ducks were acting proved otherwise. I watched them swimming anxiously about. They cackled and flapped their wings to show how nervous they were.

Hiding in the cave was very frustrating. We had work to do outside. And our animals didn’t like being locked up indoors. Still, for the sake of safety, we had to stay in. It was Grizzle, our faithful donkey, who finally saved us. Unfortunately, it was at great cost to him.

By the third day, we needed more hay for the animals. We never kept much in the cave. All of the animals’ food was at the farm. I decided to send the animals to the farm under Fritz’s guidance. After I tied them all together, Fritz would lead them away.

The next morning, I began tying the animals in a line. My wife opened the door before I was finished. Grizzle was eager to get out. He suddenly ran out of the cave and galloped straight for the marsh!

We called him back in vain. Fritz would have rushed after him, had I not held him back. In another moment, the snake reared up and spotted Grizzle. The dark, deadly jaws opened wide. Poor Grizzle’s fate was sealed.

Swift and straight, the boa was upon him. It wound around him several times, squeezing the poor donkey tighter and tighter. All the while, the giant reptile avoided the kicks of the agonised animal.

“Shoot him, Father! Shoot him now! Save our poor Grizzle!” the boys cried out.

“It is impossible, dear children! I’m afraid that Grizzle is lost to us forever. But I still have hopes that we can kill the snake!”

“But surely the snake is not going to swallow a whole donkey all at once, Father? That would be impossible!”

“Snakes have no teeth, only fangs,” I explained. “Since they cannot chew their food, they must swallow it whole. First the boa squeezes its victim into a shapeless mass, crushing the bones. Then it begins to swallow its prey. But once it swallows Grizzle’s body, the boa will be unable to move. For some time, it will use its energy for digestion. That’s when we can kill him.”

We watched in horror as the snake killed and ate Grizzle. It took the boa five hours to get Grizzle’s body down its throat! Then the swollen snake lay stiff and still along the edge of the marsh.

The time for attack was now or never! My sons and I crept out of our cave. We shot the snake dead.

“Can we eat that snake?” Franz asked. “Of course not!” cried his mother. “Why, don’t you know that snakes are poisonous, child? It would be too dangerous.”

“No, my dear wife,” I said. “The boa is not poisonous. It kills its prey by crushing it. But even if it were poisonous, we could still eat it. We would just have to throw away the head, where the poison is stored.”

The rest of that sad afternoon was spent recovering Grizzle’s remains. We were determined to give our faithful donkey a proper burial. After all, he had saved our lives at the cost of his own.

The sun was going down as we skinned and stuffed the boa. We put the skin in the cave by the entrance to our library. Over the door, we wrote these words: No donkeys admitted here.

The double meaning of this sentence pleased us all.

📝 Summary

The family is sitting outside Rock House when Fritz notices a strange movement in the distance. The narrator uses his spyglass and discovers that it is a huge boa constrictor moving towards them. He quickly orders everyone, including the animals, to go inside the cave. The family closes the door and windows and waits for the snake to pass.

The snake eventually disappears into the reedy marsh, but the family remains afraid because they know it may still be nearby. For three days, they stay inside the cave. The nervous behaviour of the ducks and geese confirms that the snake is still in the area.

On the third day, the family needs more hay from the farm. While the narrator is preparing the animals, Grizzle the donkey suddenly runs out of the cave and heads towards the marsh. The boa attacks him, wraps around him, and squeezes him tightly. The family is horrified, but the narrator knows that the snake will become weak and unable to move after swallowing its prey.

The boa takes five hours to swallow Grizzle. When the snake becomes swollen and still, the narrator and his sons leave the cave and shoot it. After killing the snake, the family gives Grizzle a proper burial because he has saved their lives at the cost of his own.

Finally, they skin and stuff the boa and place its skin near the entrance to their library. They write “No donkeys admitted here” above the door. The sentence has a humorous double meaning and helps the family end a tragic experience with a small moment of humour.

👨‍👩‍👦 Characters

👨 Narrator / Father

The family leader who stays calm, makes careful decisions and leads the plan to kill the boa.

👦 Fritz

The son who first notices the snake and helps guide the animals.

👩 Mother

She supports the family and shows concern for their safety and the animals.

🫏 Grizzle

The faithful donkey whose death becomes a sacrifice that helps save the family.

👦 Franz

The child who asks whether the family can eat the snake after it is killed.

👦 Jack and Ernest

The sons who share the danger and help their father kill the boa.

🐍 Boa Constrictor

A huge snake that threatens the family and kills Grizzle by squeezing him.

💡 Main Themes

1. Courage in the Face of Danger

The family remains calm and makes careful decisions while facing a deadly snake.

2. Sacrifice and Loyalty

Grizzle becomes a symbol of sacrifice. His actions ultimately help protect the family.

3. The Power of Nature

The boa shows how powerful and unpredictable the natural world can be.

4. Loss and Respect for Life

The family mourns Grizzle and gives him a proper burial to show their respect and gratitude.

5. Humour in Tragedy

The words “No donkeys admitted here” create a humorous double meaning after a tragic event.

📚 Vocabulary

coiled
Twisted into loops or spirals.
spyglass
A small telescope used for seeing distant objects.
boa constrictor
A large non-poisonous snake that kills its prey by squeezing it.
prey
An animal hunted or killed by another animal for food.
suspense
A feeling of excitement or anxious uncertainty.
agony / agonized
Extreme physical or mental pain.
shapeless mass
Something with no clear or normal shape.
reared up
Raised the front part of the body upright.
swollen
Made larger or puffed up, usually because of pressure or injury.
fangs
Long, sharp teeth, especially in snakes or other animals.
burial
The act of placing a dead body in the ground.
double meaning
A phrase that has two different interpretations.
skinned
Had the skin removed from an animal.
marsh
An area of wet, soft land, often covered with grass or reeds.
faithful
Loyal and dependable.
galloped
Ran very fast, especially like a horse.
frustrated
Upset or annoyed because something cannot be done.
digestion
The process of breaking down food inside the body.
remains
What is left of something after it has been destroyed or after death.
admitted
Allowed to enter a place.

B. Inference and Analytical Questions

26. What does the narrator’s leadership during the snake threat reveal about his character?
It shows that he is calm, rational and able to make careful decisions during danger.
27. How does the family’s behaviour show teamwork and unity during the crisis?
They followed instructions, stayed together, protected one another and worked together to defeat the snake.
28. In what ways is Grizzle shown as a heroic animal?
He is shown as faithful and brave, and his actions ultimately help protect the family.
29. How does the family show respect and gratitude to Grizzle after his death?
They give him a proper burial and remember his sacrifice.
30. How does the chapter build suspense?
It uses the slow approach of the snake, the family’s three-day wait, the nervous animals and the dramatic attack on Grizzle.
31. What does the narrator’s explanation about eating snakes reveal about his knowledge and practicality?
It shows that he is knowledgeable, practical and able to use facts to solve survival problems.
32. How do the children show maturity and courage during the crisis?
They follow their father’s instructions, remain with the family and show concern for Grizzle.
33. What values are shown by the family during and after the attack?
Courage, unity, respect for life, loyalty, gratitude and practical thinking.
34. How is humour used at the end of the chapter to ease tension?
The family uses the double meaning of “No donkeys admitted here” to create a light moment after the tragedy.
35. What deeper meaning might lie in placing the snake’s skin near the library?
It can represent survival, the family’s experience and the memory of Grizzle’s sacrifice.
