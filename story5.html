📖 Reading — Improving Our Home

The next morning, my wife asked me to plant the little fruit trees. She had watered them and spread earth over their roots, but she could not plant them herself. I told her that she had already done enough. Then the three older boys and I spent the day planting the trees. We used bamboo stalks to support the young trunks.

After we had finished, I suggested that we should make coops for the hens and pigeons. We could use gourds and hang them in the trees, where they would be safe from jackals. We decided to leave this project until later.

We spent the next week making many trips to the wreck. We removed every useful thing that was left on the ship.

Now that we had taken everything useful from the wreck, I decided that we should sink the ship. The next day, Fritz, Jack, Ernest and I sailed out to it. We placed a cask of gunpowder on the deck, lit it and sailed away. By the time we reached the shore, the ship exploded.

💥 The destruction of the wreck made their isolation seem final.

We were all very sad. The thought that we might never see Switzerland again made us wonder what the future would bring.

The next morning, however, our sadness changed to delight. The beach was covered with planks and boards from the ruined ship. There was now a great deal of useful timber for us to use.

We decided that it was time to explore the interior of the island. We wanted to discover what dangers might be there and what useful things we could find.

We prepared carefully. The cow and Grizzle, the donkey, pulled a sled loaded with equipment. We took a tent, food and ammunition for several days. Our dogs, Turk and Juno, followed us.

As we travelled through the forests, we discovered many unusual trees. One was the guava tree, whose fruit looked rather like an apple. We also found the candleberry tree. Its berries could be boiled to produce wax for candles.

Fritz was the first to notice some India rubber trees. Our father punctured one of the trees and caught the milky sap in a gourd. We could use this material to make waterproof boots and shoes.

We also found oak trees with sweet acorns. Ernest tasted some cocoa beans and thought they were delicious. He filled his pockets with them because they could be used to make a tasty chocolate drink.

“These can be used to make a tasty chocolate drink!”

We stopped for the night and prepared supper. Grizzle was grazing nearby when suddenly his ears pricked up. He brayed and ran away. We sent the dogs after him, but they soon returned without the donkey.

Darkness fell, so we stopped searching. Our father was worried that Grizzle might have sensed a fierce wild animal. He said nothing to frighten us, but he made a large fire to keep any dangerous animals away.

The next morning, Grizzle was still missing. Father, Jack, Turk and Juno went searching for him while Fritz and Ernest stayed with their mother for protection.

They followed Grizzle's hoofprints. It seemed that he had joined a herd, but eventually they lost the trail.

Jack wanted to continue searching. Soon they discovered a herd of buffalo. The animals stared at them. They began to move away, but the dogs suddenly rushed forward and attacked a young buffalo calf.

The whole herd turned and charged. Father fired his gun and killed the leader. The buffalo then ran away.

The dogs were still fighting the young buffalo. They could not bring it down, so Father decided that they should save it and tame it as a beast of burden.

Jack used a lasso to catch the animal by its hind legs. Father told Jack to help him hold the front legs and head. He then made a cut inside the animal's nose and passed a rope through it.

🐃 The young buffalo was painful to handle, but the family hoped to tame it as a replacement for Grizzle.

When the young buffalo was free, Father pulled the rope gently. The animal followed without resisting.

Father then cut two large steaks from the dead buffalo. He packed them in salt and left the rest of the meat for the dogs.

On the way home, the dogs ran ahead. Suddenly they began to yelp. A jackal had attacked them. Father realized that there must be a young jackal nearby.

The dogs defeated the jackal. Jack then asked if they could take the young jackal home. He named it Fangs. The other children were delighted. They felt that the new animals were a good exchange for losing their donkey.

That evening, the family enjoyed the buffalo steaks. Ernest told them about a young eagle he had seen. Father suggested that they should capture it and train it to hunt like a falcon.

The next day, Ernest threw a cloth over the eagle's head and caught it.

The following day, they returned to Falcon's Nest. Father decided that the young buffalo could replace Grizzle and pull the sled. Ernest carried the blindfolded eagle.

On their journey, they stopped to collect candleberry berries for candles and India rubber sap for waterproof shoes.

When they arrived home, Mother asked Father to build a staircase inside the tree house. The rope ladder was difficult for her to climb.

They discovered that bees lived inside the tree. Father believed that the tree was hollow. He used smoke to calm the bees, removed the bees and their hives, and covered the holes in the trunk so the bees could not return.

They moved the bees into hollow gourds and collected the honey.

Father then began turning the inside of the tree into a staircase. He cut an opening for the captain's cabin door and hung the door with hinges.

After clearing away the rotten wood, he placed the trunk of a young tree inside as the centre of the spiral staircase. He cut notches in both the young and old trees and fitted and nailed wooden steps into them.

He also cut windows to provide light and fresh air.

It took a full month, but eventually they had a complete staircase from the ground floor to the top of the tree house.

🏠 The new staircase made their home safer, more comfortable and easier to use.

Meanwhile, the animals were multiplying. The pig had seven piglets, the hens had forty chicks, the goats had two kids and the sheep had five lambs. Juno also had puppies and accepted the baby jackal.

With the animals producing young and the garden growing well, the family's food supply seemed secure.

Father also made shoes for everyone. He filled socks with sand and covered them with clay to make moulds. When the clay hardened, he covered the moulds with layers of India rubber, allowing each layer to dry.

After the rubber had dried in the sun, he broke the clay moulds and attached buffalo hide soles. He then brushed the hide with India rubber to make the boots waterproof.

Finally, Father planned a fresh-water system. He built a dam upstream and used bamboo pipes to carry water to a turtle shell. Extra water flowed through the hole made by the harpoon.

💧 The new water system saved the family from carrying water from the stream every day.

📝 Chapter Summary

In this chapter, the family works hard to improve their home and make their life on the island easier. They plant young fruit trees and plan to build safe coops for their hens and pigeons.

After removing everything useful from the wreck, Father decides to destroy the ship. Although its destruction makes their isolation seem final, the sea soon brings useful timber from the wreck onto the beach.

The family then explores the interior of the island. They discover guava, candleberry, India rubber, oak and cocoa. These natural resources provide food and materials for candles, chocolate drinks and waterproof shoes.

During the expedition, Grizzle disappears. The family eventually discovers a herd of buffalo. After the dogs attack a young buffalo, Father kills the leader and decides to tame the young animal as a beast of burden.

The family also adopts a baby jackal named Fangs, and Ernest captures a young eagle that Father plans to train for hunting.

Back at home, the family improves the tree house by building a staircase inside the tree. They also move bees into hollow gourds and collect their honey.

The animals produce many young, increasing the family's food supply. Father also makes waterproof boots using clay, India rubber and buffalo hide. Finally, he builds a water system using a dam and bamboo pipes, making life much easier for everyone.

💡 Main Theme of the Chapter

Adaptation, Resourcefulness and Improving Life

The main idea of this chapter is that the family does not simply survive on the island. They continually find ways to make their home safer, more comfortable and more efficient.

They use natural resources such as bamboo, India rubber, candleberries, timber and clay. They also learn to manage animals, grow food and create systems that save time and effort.

🔧 Resourcefulness

The family finds practical uses for the natural resources around them.

🌱 Improving Life

They improve their home, food supply, water system and transport.

🤝 Teamwork

Every member of the family contributes useful skills and ideas.

🐾 Animal Management

The family learns to protect, tame and use animals responsibly.

💡 Problem Solving

Problems are solved through careful planning and practical thinking.

🏠 Adaptation

The family adapts to island life instead of simply waiting to be rescued.
