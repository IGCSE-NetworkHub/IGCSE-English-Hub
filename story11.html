📖 Reading — Home Again

After a long journey, the family returned to Rock House with a blindfolded ostrich. The journey took two days. On their way home, they visited their farm and were delighted to find that their pigs, goats and chickens had increased in number. They also noticed that the antelope herd had settled peacefully near their land. They brought chickens back with them.

When they arrived at Rock House, they opened the doors and windows to let in fresh air. They tied the ostrich between two bamboo posts. However, taming the bird proved difficult. For an entire week, it remained wild and would not allow anyone to approach it.

The father eventually used smoke, the same method he had used to tame the eagle. The ostrich became calm, but refused to eat. For three days, it grew weaker and weaker. The mother fed it balls made from cornflour and butter. Gradually, the bird recovered and began eating rice, guavas, corn and small pebbles.

Franz was surprised to see the ostrich eating pebbles. His father explained that birds swallow small stones to help them digest their food. The father then invented a special hood with movable blinkers to guide the ostrich. He also designed a saddle and reins to control its movement and speed.

The family named the ostrich Hurricane because of its speed and power. Eventually, everyone learned to manage and ride it. Their life at Rock House became more comfortable as the family grew crops such as wheat, barley, corn, potatoes and other vegetables.

The father also began making hats from rat skin and India rubber, producing a material similar to felt. He dyed the hats red, shaped them on a wooden block and decorated them with ostrich feathers. The mother helped with the silk linings. Everyone liked the first cap, which was made for Franz.

Ten years had passed on the island. The father reflected proudly on how his sons had grown. Fritz, aged twenty-five, was strong and active. Ernest, aged twenty-three, was mild, calm and studious. Jack, aged twenty, was graceful and witty. Franz, aged eighteen, combined the qualities of his brothers and had a good sense of humour. They had become kind, loving and God-fearing young men.

📝 Summary

After a long journey, the family returned home to Rock House, bringing with them the blindfolded ostrich. Along the way, they visited their farm and were happy to find that their animals, including pigs, goats and chickens, had increased in number. They also noticed that the antelope herd had settled peacefully near their territory. Once home, they opened their cave home and secured the ostrich between two bamboo posts.

Taming the ostrich proved difficult. For an entire week, the bird remained wild and untouchable. The narrator then used the same smoke technique that had worked on taming the eagle. Although the ostrich calmed down, it refused to eat. His wife fed it buttered cornflour balls, which helped the ostrich regain its appetite and strength. Over time, the bird responded to training. The narrator invented a special hood and a saddle with reins to control its movement. They named him Hurricane, and soon the entire family learned how to ride and guide him.

The family's life became stable and productive. Their land yielded crops like wheat, barley, corn and potatoes. The narrator also found time for hobbies, such as crafting hats from rat skin. He created a felt-like material using India rubber, dyed it red and decorated the hats with ostrich feathers.

Finally, the chapter reflects on the passing of time. The family had now lived on the island for ten years. The boys had grown into fine young men, each with different strengths but all united in love for one another and their parents.
